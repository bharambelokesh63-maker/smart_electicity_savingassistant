Shared scheduling constraints (used by Best-First, A*, Hill Climbing and CSP)
Every algorithm reads your saved appliances and tariff, so results change when your data changes. Tip: set a low capacity (for example 2.5 kW) to see backtracking and capacity rejections in action.
Best-First Search (greedy)

States are partial schedules. A priority queue always expands the state with the lowest heuristic h(n) = sum of each unplaced appliance's cheapest possible placement (ties broken by lower g). Fast, but with no optimality guarantee.

A* Search

Same state space, priority f(n) = g(n) + h(n), where g is the real accumulated cost (energy cost + soft penalty) and h is admissible and consistent. Goal: every flexible appliance placed with all hard constraints satisfied. Optimal for the modelled objective when the search finishes within the expansion limit.

Hill Climbing (steepest descent on cost)

Starts from your original schedule (or a CSP-repaired valid one), then repeatedly moves the single appliance that lowers the objective the most. Stops at a local optimum or at the iteration limit.

Constraint Satisfaction with Backtracking

Variables = flexible appliances; domains = permitted start hours; constraints = window, duration, unavailable hours and hourly capacity. Recursive backtracking with Minimum-Remaining-Values variable selection.

Forward Chaining (rule-based inference)

Facts are asserted from your appliance data; IF-THEN rules fire repeatedly until no new fact can be derived. Every firing is logged.

Bayesian Network (household consumption category)

A 4-node network: UsageIntensity, HighPowerCount and StandbyLevel are parents of ConsumptionCategory. Exact inference by enumeration. Evidence is derived from your appliances, or you can set it manually. Educational estimate under the stated assumptions - not a measurement or forecast.
